parser: the shared parser read month first

Every tenant on file writes the day first. The shared parser read every date month first, so a booking for 2 December landed on 12 February. Engineering fixed it on 5 October (fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales). The count includes the reports the desk read as one-day moves, because the desk files them here by tenant. Tracked in umarvinci/support-kb#1, closed as fixed.

hint: booking-v3 asked for the month first

booking-v3 printed MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. Its tenants write the day first, so their people typed the month first, as the hint told them, and the form read the date day first. The parser fix doesn’t touch this. Engineering won’t fix it: the booking-v3 form is retired, so no new report can come in through it. Tracked in umarvinci/support-kb#2, closed as won’t fix.

shift: a date moves by one day

Some reports describe a date moved by one day, or one meeting on different days for people in different places: Tuesday in Lisbon, Monday in Toronto. A month/day swap can’t do that, so the parser fix won’t stop it. The desk noted these as one-day moves in desk-notes.json and counts them under parser by tenant; they are also their own problem. They point at time zones; the cause isn’t confirmed yet. Tracked in umarvinci/support-kb#3, still open.

Why one ticket hid them

  • Decision 0001 merges reports whose subject lines match. Every problem here produces the same subjects (“Wrong day on the invite”, “Month and day swapped?”), so the subject can’t tell them apart.
  • Intake’s tag is a guess from the reporter’s browser, not from the tenant, so it doesn’t separate them either.
  • What separates them is where a report comes from (its tenant and booking form) and what it says happened (a swap, or a one-day move). problems.ts makes that split, and the table above is built from it when the page renders.
